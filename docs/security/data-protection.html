Data Protection

Comprehensive data classification frameworks, encryption standards, PII handling policies, and data retention guidelines.

Data Classification Framework

Restricted

Highly sensitive data requiring maximum protection. Unauthorized access could cause severe damage.

Examples: PII, PHI, Payment Cards, Trade Secrets, Passwords

Confidential

Sensitive business information with limited distribution. Disclosure could harm business interests.

Examples: Financial Reports, Contracts, Employee Records, Strategic Plans

Internal

Information for internal use only. Not intended for external distribution but not highly sensitive.

Examples: Policies, Procedures, Internal Memos, Training Materials

Public

Information approved for public release. No adverse impact from disclosure.

Examples: Marketing Materials, Press Releases, Public Documentation

PII Categories & Handling

Definition

Personally Identifiable Information (PII) is any data that could potentially identify a specific individual, either alone or combined with other data.

Direct Identifiers (High Sensitivity)

Social Security Number
Passport Number
Credit Card Numbers
Biometric Data
Medical Records
Driver's License

Indirect Identifiers (Medium Sensitivity)

Email Address
Phone Number
Physical Address
Date of Birth
IP Address
Employment Info

Data Retention Schedule

Financial Records 7 Years

Tax records, invoices, audit trails. Required for SOX compliance and tax regulations.

Employee Records 7 Years after termination

Personnel files, performance reviews, payroll records. Required for employment law compliance.

Customer Data 3 Years after last activity

Account information, transaction history. Subject to GDPR data minimization principles.

Security Logs 1 Year minimum

Access logs, authentication events, security incidents. Required for forensic analysis.

Legal Documents Permanent

Contracts, licenses, regulatory filings. Retain for duration of business plus wind-down period.

Marketing Data Until consent withdrawn

Marketing preferences, email subscriptions. Requires active consent under GDPR.

72-Hour Rule

Data breaches must be reported to supervisory authority within 72 hours of awareness.

Privacy by Design

Build privacy protections into systems from the start, not as an afterthought.

DPO Requirement

Appoint a Data Protection Officer for large-scale processing of sensitive data.

Cross-Border Transfers

Use approved mechanisms (SCCs, BCRs, adequacy decisions) for international transfers.

Data Protection Best Practices

Data Minimization

Collect only the data you need. Less data means less risk. Regularly review and purge unnecessary data.

Anonymization

When possible, anonymize or pseudonymize data to reduce privacy risks while maintaining utility.

Access Control

Implement least-privilege access. Users should only access data necessary for their role.

Data Inventory

Maintain comprehensive records of what data you have, where it's stored, and who can access it.

Encrypt Everything

Use strong encryption for data at rest and in transit. Manage keys securely with proper rotation.

Monitor & Audit

Log all access to sensitive data. Regular audits ensure compliance and detect anomalies.
